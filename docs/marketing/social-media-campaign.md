## 1. Background and rationale `[CHANGED]`

> *Before:* scope described as "email triage + referral processing".
> *After:* scope broadened to **email triage + any inbound medical PDF** — referrals, pathology results, radiology results, specialist letters, consent forms, and other clinical documents — because the product (PDF → structured data → PMS) is the same regardless of document type.

Medical specialist practices in Australia spend a disproportionate share of clinical and administrative time on two tightly coupled inbound workflows:

- **Email triage** — sorting and routing patient, GP, hospital, pathology, radiology, and supplier email into the right queue, often by a single front-of-house staffer.
- **Inbound medical document processing** — extracting structured data (sender, patient, indication, urgency, results, attachments) from **any inbound clinical PDF** — GP referrals, specialist letters, pathology results, radiology reports, consent forms — and creating or updating a record in the practice management system. The PDFs typically arrive as email attachments, scanned faxes, or direct uploads; the AI handles them all the same way.

These two workflows are the most pragmatic place to start an AI rollout in a medical SME because:

1. They are high-volume, repetitive, and rules-driven — ideal AI surface area.
2. They have a measurable cost (FTE hours, error rate, time-to-first-contact) — easy ROI story.
3. They sit at the front door of the practice, so improvements compound across the rest of the business (faster bookings, fewer missed referrals or results, better patient experience).
4. They do not require the AI to make clinical decisions — keeping the deployment inside a low-risk regulatory band for a first project.

Treating email triage and inbound document automation as a **single combined workflow** for marketing purposes (rather than two products, or one product per document type) keeps the message simple, the demo concrete, and the deployment fast.

## 2. The social-media pivot — what's different about this revision `[NEW]`

The original campaign treated paid social as one of several channels. This revision treats **social media as the primary acquisition engine**, for three reasons:

1. **Where the audience actually is.** Specialist practice owners, principals, and practice managers are reachable on LinkedIn (decision makers) and on Meta (front-desk and practice managers). Healthcare-ops content travels well on both.
2. **Brand-building doubles as lead-gen.** A productised AI offer in healthcare needs trust before it needs urgency. Founder-led social content (Andrew Lai) builds both at once — it earns the eligibility call rather than buying it cold.
3. **Compounding creative beats one-off outbound.** A 27-variant creative matrix refreshed every two weeks generates more, better, cheaper qualified leads than a manual outbound campaign — and produces case-study and PR raw material as a by-product.

Outbound, PR, and partnerships still exist — they just stop being the *engine*. They orbit a central, always-on social presence.

### 8.6 Organic content engine `[NEW]`

#### Content pillars (4 + 1)

1. **Pain stories** — front-desk-reality vignettes. *"It's 9:42 a.m. and there are 47 unread emails…"* — pulls in the reader who lives that day. Source: real practice manager interviews + Andrew's notes.
2. **Numbers / proof** — quantified outcomes. *"Practice X saved 11 hours / week in their first 30 days."* Source: pilot practices.
3. **Behind-the-scenes** — the build, the work session, integration screenshots (no PHI). Builds trust and shows the system is real.
4. **Founder POV** — Andrew's take on AI in medical practice. *"AI shouldn't make clinical decisions. Here's the line we won't cross."* Trust + brand-building.
5. **Customer voice** — testimonials, quote graphics, short clips (only after first paying practice). Highest-converting paid creative.
